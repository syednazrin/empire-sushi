## What The Market Might Be Missing 👀

- Empire isn’t just “another sushi chain” — **financials and store-level data support a digital-ready, mall-anchored QSR story**.  
- **Mall concentration** is a feature, not a bug — better traffic and brand visibility.  
- **Saturation and competitive-area metrics** show where Empire is underpenetrated vs where it’s already dense.  
- **Menu and pricing analytics** (from the project) allow direct comparison vs Sushi King, Mentai, etc. — margin leadership is consistent with positioning.

## Risks ⚠️

- **Execution & monetisation** — Data supports the story; delivery depends on ops and same-store growth.  
- **Competition** — Family Mart (481 outlets) and others have scale; format and margin matter.  
- **Policy / real estate** — Mall mix and govt initiatives (e.g. digital, tourism) can shift economics.  
- **Not the most hyped amongst brands** — Google Trends / search interest may show Empire isn’t #1 in relative search vs Sushi King, Zanmai, or others. That could mean: **weaker top-of-mind**, slower word-of-mouth and viral pull, or need for **higher marketing spend** to compensate. Younger and more social-media-driven cohorts often gravitate to “hyped” brands; if Empire under-indexes on search and buzz, same-store growth and new-store pull could lag over time.  
- **Mall dependency** — 82% in-mall = traffic leverage but also **reliance on footfall**. If mall traffic softens or shifts (e.g. hybrid work, different weekend behaviour), Empire is more exposed than a brand with a stronger off-mall or delivery-led pull.  
- **Brand vs distribution** — Strong financials and footprint can hide a **brand-awareness gap**. If the story is “we’re in the right places” but search and sentiment lag peers, long-term rerating may be capped until brand heat catches up.

### Slide 6 — Search by store (Google Trends)
- **What the slide shows:** **Relative search interest over time** by brand (Malaysia), from Google Trends — time series by store/brand with an **average interest** number per brand in the legend. So you see a line per brand (Empire, Sushi King, Zanmai, Mentai, Nippon, Jiro, etc.) and can compare who is searched more over the period.

Geography = **Malaysia** only. Time range = as per the CSVs (e.g. Feb 2025–Jan 2026 per Slide 2 assumptions). So the slide answers “Empire vs Sushi King vs others in search interest,” not “X million searches.” If Empire’s line or **avg** is below Sushi King or Zanmai, the data says Empire is **not the most searched** in this set — that’s the “not the most hyped” risk; if at or above, it supports stronger brand heat.
- **Opinion:** This is the **only slide that measures demand signal** (search) rather than stores, financials, or menu. Use it to stress-test the story: strong revenue and margin can still face weaker top-of-mind or buzz. If Empire’s average or trend line lags Sushi King or Zanmai, flag it as “not the most hyped” and consider what that implies for marketing spend and same-store growth. If Empire leads or tracks the pack, the data supports brand heat and the overall narrative holds.
