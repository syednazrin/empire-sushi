## 1. Spatial & brand analytics 🗺️

*Data: 918 store locations across 9 brands, mapped by lat/lng and enriched with state/district; 906 stores classified as in-mall or not; competitive areas = stores with ≥1 competitor within 1 km; saturation = brand’s stores per district vs national average; market gap = districts with zero Empire but high competitor count.*

**Numbers:**

- **Total stores in dataset:** 918  
- **Empire Sushi:** 141 outlets (vs Sushi King 123, Sushi Zanmai 40, Sushi Mentai 51, Nippon 20, Sushi Jiro 13, Sushi Plus 14, Aeon 35, Family Mart 481).  
  → Empire has the **largest sushi-focused footprint** among comparable peers (141 vs next 123).

- **Mall presence (classified stores):**  
  - Market: **383 of 906** stores in malls = **42%**.  
  - Empire: **115 of 141** stores in malls = **82%**.  
  → Empire is **~2x more mall-concentrated** than the market → premium, high-traffic placement.

- **Competitive areas:** Count of store locations that have **≥1 competitor within 1 km** (shown as blue zones on map).  
  → Shows where Empire (and others) operate in **crowded** vs less contested areas.

- **Saturation score:** (Empire’s stores per district ÷ national stores per district) × 50, capped at 100%.  
  → Tells you if Empire is **over-concentrated** (high %) or **under-penetrated** (lower %) in the districts it’s in.

- **Market gap:** List of **districts with zero Empire presence** but **high competitor presence**.  
  → **Expansion pipeline** — where to open next without over-saturating.

**Why it matters for the thesis:** Geography says Empire is in the right places (mall-heavy), has room to grow (market gap), and we can see crowdedness (competitive areas) and concentration (saturation). All from the map and spatial metrics.

## 2. Pricing & menu items 📊

*Data: Item-level menu (sushi only): store, item, price, category. Charts: box plot (price range by brand), histogram (price buckets), scatter (items vs price), category-wise average price. Brands: Empire, Sushi King, Mentai, etc.*

**What the data shows:**

- **Scope:** Sushi items only (no ramen/rice bowls) so pricing is **like-for-like** across brands.
- **Box plot:** Price range (min, Q1, median, Q3, max) by brand → where **Empire’s prices sit vs Sushi King, Mentai, Zanmai** (premium vs value).
- **Histogram:** Count of items in price bands (e.g. RM 0–5, 5–10, …) by brand → **price mix** (e.g. more mid-tier vs budget).
- **Category-wise average:** Average price by category (e.g. nigiri, rolls) by brand → **where Empire is premium or efficient** by category.
- **Scatter:** Each point = one menu item (price on y-axis) → **spread and outliers** by brand.

**Why it matters for the thesis:** Empire’s **16.1% PAT margin** (financials) can come from **premium pricing** or **efficiency**. This slide gives the **pricing side**: if Empire’s distribution is right of peers, margin is partly price; if left, it’s efficiency. The data is in the deck — box plot, histogram, category view — and justifies the margin story with item-level numbers.

## 4. Search by store (Google Trends) 📈

*Data: Relative search interest over time by brand (Malaysia). Time series + average interest per brand in the deck.*

**What the data shows:**

- **Metric:** Relative (indexed) search interest — **not** absolute search volume.  
- **Use:** Compare **Empire vs Sushi King, Zanmai, Mentai**, etc. over the period.  
- **If Empire’s line is below peers** → data says Empire is **not the most searched** in this set → **risk**: weaker top-of-mind, may need more marketing or time to build buzz.  
- **If Empire’s line is at or above peers** → data supports **stronger brand heat** and aligns with revenue/margin story.

**Why it matters for the thesis:** Spatial + financials + menu say “we’re big, we’re in malls, we have margin.” Search says **“how much do people look for us?”** — the only demand-signal slide. So we **justify the thesis** with store count, mall %, revenue, margin, and pricing; we **flag risk** with search if Empire under-indexes. All from the project’s Search-by-store slide.
